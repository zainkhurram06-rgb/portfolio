JARVIS runs the team and measures it against your target: about $500 a week now, one website a day after the first month. 2. The personalities

Each one is a plain document you can edit. The "Brief" at the top goes into every AI prompt that agent sends.

FRIDAY deepestWarm, quick, honest AI caller. One goal per call: they agree to look at the demo and say where to send it. Six-step call shape, how to reach the owner, answers to 14 common objections, buying signals, and a hard "you never" list.
EDITHPrecise, patient researcher. Never lowers the bar, never guesses a profile, read-only in your browser.

HAPPYThe owner's eyes before the owner sees it. Blocks what's wrong, warns about what's weak, explains every fail in one line.
JARVISNumbers first, points at the bottleneck, only asks you for what only you can do. Targets in targets.json.

3. Hand these to Cowork

Both prompts are in COWORK-PROMPTS.md, ready to paste.

Job 1: training research start this first, it takes hours

Cowork watches Saad Sells and other real cold calls to small businesses and turns them into FRIDAY's techniques, objection answers, openers and trade pain points. It also studies the best real websites per trade for MARK, lead signals per country for EDITH, and a quality checklist for HAPPY. Everything is in its own words with a source for each item, and pressure tactics go on a "never do" list. Files land in training/, and the agents pick them up automatically: FRIDAY's prompt, MARK's writing and HAPPY's report all include them.

Job 2: the Google key for EDITH you add the card

Cowork creates the project, switches on Places API (New), makes a key locked to that one API, sets a $10 budget alert, and saves the key in .env. It stops at the billing step for you, and never sees or types payment details.

4. Then the first real run

  1. Log the Claude CLI in (it reads Instagram profiles in your Chrome). Run claude, then type /login, and make sure the Claude in Chrome extension is on.
  2. Clear the sample leads: 
    .venv\Scripts\python -m agents.pipeline remove --samples
  3. EDITH on a real city, live (PowerShell, in C:\Users\zaink\portfolio\jarvis-agents): 
    $env:EDITH_PLACES = "live"; $env:EDITH_SOCIAL = "live"; .venv\Scripts\python -m agents.edith find --niche salon --city Dubai --country AE
    She stays inside Google's free 1,000 searches a month, reads at most 120 profiles a day, and carries on where she stopped the next time you run it.
  4. See what she used: 
    .venv\Scripts\python -m agents.edith usage

5. What only you can decide

  • Your price. FRIDAY can't quote one until price_text is set in agents/friday/rules.json, and JARVIS assumes $500 a site in targets.json. Also needed there: company_name, the name she says on calls.
  • The daily ceiling. One sale a day means about 81 leads, 73 demos and 56 calls a day, at the starting guesses in targets.json (12% of owners reached are interested, 30% of those buy). Today MARK's research is capped at 25 a day and FRIDAY at 40 calls a day to protect your accounts. JARVIS flags this as the bottleneck once the numbers start moving. Raising those caps safely is the next big job.
  • ElevenLabs and Twilio for FRIDAY's first real test call to your phone.

What changed in the code

  • EDITH: places.py (Google Places with map tiles, usage and cost tracking), social.py (Codex finds profiles, Claude in Chrome reads them, daily caps), edith.py (cities can run over several days), new samples in samples/edith/. Apify is removed.
  • agents/persona.py loads personalities and Cowork's training. Keys are read from .env.
  • FRIDAY's prompt is built from pitch.md, her personality and the playbook. After a call she now also records the owner's name, how customers find them, and how keen they were.
  • MARK's prompt now includes his personality and the per-trade research. HAPPY's report lists the extra checks to do by hand.
  • JARVIS: python -m agents.jarvis run | status | report.
